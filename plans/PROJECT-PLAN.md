| [DRIVING-AND-PLACE.md](DRIVING-AND-PLACE.md) | planned | Seven things between the game as it is and the game it is meant to be, gathered from playing it. **A bake defect first**: a bore does not bore — the mountain over it is pushed down to road level instead, so a tunnel is a cutting and the skyline is wrong; Beacon spends 1774 m of its 3 km inside the hill, which is where it shows worst. **Road diversity is half-arrived**: the two-lane profile and the climbing lane are in every baked road, and intersections do not exist anywhere in the codebase — so the question is partly "why can nobody see the lanes" and partly "that was never built". **Traffic** ends up in the ditch often enough to be the first thing a player notices, and drives into a collision without ever trying to avoid it. **The game is silent** — no tyres, no motor, no birdsong, no water. **The simplified control scheme** — indicate an attempt to pass rather than steer — is designed in CONTROL-SCHEMES.md and absent from the game. And **the editor has never been used in anger** to build a track somebody wanted, which is the only test of whether it can. |
| [TRACK-PACKS.md](TRACK-PACKS.md) | planned | What a track costs, how a set of them is defined and built, and what makes them different. A default bake is **57 MB** on disk (48 MB compressed, 22 s) — and 26 MB of it is the tree art, byte-identical between seeds, so four tracks is one shared art pack plus roughly 31 MB each rather than 4 × 57 MB. Tracks become committed `glisteel/tracks/*.toml` recipes read by `glisteel-bake --recipe`, because `ProceduralWorld` takes about twenty parameters and the command line passes six: `closed`, `route`, `water_level`, `relief`, `maximum_bank`, `variety`, `channels`, `structures` and `posted` are all built and unreachable. Four launch tracks on that — **Ashdown** (the measured circuit, 7.2 km with 895 m of bridge and 289 m of bore), **Tidewater** (the valleys flooded), **Beacon** (point-to-point hill climb), **Steelbowl** (banked oval) — published as release artifacts on this repository's own `content-v1` tag, with `glisteel/assets/` becoming the base pack fetched on first run. Downloaded through the engine's [CONTENT-PACKS.md](https://github.com/mcfletch/openglcontext/blob/main/plans/CONTENT-PACKS.md) facility. Blocked on `glisteel-editor` not being on PyPI, which the release workflow needs to bake with. |
| [2026-08-23-CRASH-ON-CONTACT.md](2026-08-23-CRASH-ON-CONTACT.md) | landed (2026-08-23) | Driving square into the back of a traffic car at speed did not end the run and clipping it off centre did. The crash rule read a closing speed off the two cars' positions once a frame, after the physics had already cancelled the velocity it was reading — square-on being the case it cancels all of. A crash is contact now: `omi_physics` records how hard each pair met before it resolves them (`Contact.approach`, `PhysicsWorld.impact_on`), the rule runs inside the fixed step, and traffic bodies carry their velocity so a car ahead is a car rather than a wall. |
| [2026-08-21-TEST-QUALITY.md](2026-08-21-TEST-QUALITY.md) | landed (2026-08-21) | What the suite costs and what it says: the whole run cut from 492 s to 312 s by driving each drive once rather than once per question, the rule for what may be shared between tests (what cannot change) and what may not (anything a test writes to), the ring and call-counting helpers moved into `tests/support.py`, and three assertions that could pass on an empty road made to say so. |
| [2026-08-19-PLAY-REVIEW.md](2026-08-19-PLAY-REVIEW.md) | landed (2026-08-19) | Ten complaints from a drive of the shipped world, and what each turned out to be: a steering aid with a lane of its own, a start line the game did not read, a bonnet seen edge-on, traffic stood on its own roof, tunnel portals buried in the hillside, causeway flanks wound inside out, and a restart that dropped the car through the world. Records the one cost accepted (the hill over a long bore is opened into a cutting) and the terrain feature that would remove it. |
| [2026-08-19-CODE-REVIEW.md](2026-08-19-CODE-REVIEW.md) | P1, E, S, T and P3 landed (2026-08-20) | A gating code review of `glisteel` and `glisteel-editor` and the engine interfaces they use: ten correctness defects, a measured performance profile (75 % of an editor redraw and 25 % of a game frame spent recomputing constants), security and untrusted-input hardening, the state of the type and lint gates, and what the coverage figures hide. Priority-ordered remediation checklist at the top; the ten P1 correctness defects and the measured performance, security and gate items are fixed and pinned; §12 to §15 record what landed, what was declined and why, and what is still open. |
| [CONTROL-SCHEMES.md](CONTROL-SCHEMES.md) | switch landed, schemes on trial (2026-08-20) | Ways of driving it, for a player who has not driven it before: the control interface split into a source, a scheme and a set of assists; six schemes from lane-keeping to the pointer driving the whole car; the pointer, key and gamepad sources each available to all of them; assists and the presets that bundle them; which parts are generic vehicle control for the engine and which are glisteel's own two-lane road and race rules; and the experiment over the existing feel harness that chooses between them. §9.1 is the switch as built -- `--control` over `glisteel.schemes`, the drivable zone, and five schemes on trial -- and §8.1 the first numbers off it. The lane switch is a speed-aware manoeuvre that does the steering and nothing else, and the road carries its own speeds: Ontario-pattern caution and MAXIMUM signs from the engine, and a HUD that says so in easy mode. |
